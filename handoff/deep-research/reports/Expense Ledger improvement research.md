# Protect data first, then polish Expense Ledger

**Do the data-safety work first, fix the misleading "one-off" label and the small home-screen wins next, and refuse the two ideas that would hurt you most: automatic emailing of history and multiple decorative themes.** The research found that the best free way to stop users losing data is a "last backup" indicator plus a "Send backup to myself" button that uses the phone's own share menu (Telegram Saved Messages, WhatsApp, Gmail, Drive). This gives the benefit of "email me my history" without a server, without costs, and without breaking the "nothing leaves your device" promise. Dark theme is worth doing, but only as "follow the phone's setting"; a pile of different themes is not. Most UI items (the "+" chooser, latest transactions on Home, the all-time overview, a hideable number pad) are cheap but rest on design reasoning, not on tested evidence about users, so the report marks each as a judgment call. The biggest weakness of the whole evidence base is that **no competitor app screens were retrieved**, nothing was tested on a real phone, and **no Azerbaijani law text and no Azerbaijani user research** were reached. Those gaps are listed in the section "What we could not verify and what to check next".

How to read this report: each item has a verdict (DO NOW / DO LATER / DO NOT DO), evidence with a strength rating (Strong = official documentation or standards; Medium = reputable but secondary or vendor sources; Weak = opinion, forum posts or the research team's own reasoning), a rough cost in working days (these are estimates by the research team, not measured), risks, and a recommended position in the order. Azerbaijani and Russian wording in the notes is draft only and has not been reviewed by a native speaker.

## Data loss is mostly caused by users and Safari, so protect that first

**What really destroys a local-first ledger.** The browser keeps the ledger in storage that is "best effort" by default. According to MDN, data can disappear in three situations: the device runs short of space (the least recently used site is cleared first), the browser's maximum is exceeded, or Safari deletes it proactively ([MDN storage quotas](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria)). Chrome's own guidance says automatic clearing of regularly visited sites is rare and that users clearing data themselves is the more common cause ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/persistent-storage)). For Safari, when tracking prevention is on, script-written data can be deleted after seven days of Safari use without the user tapping the site; apps added to the Home Screen count their own days and WebKit did not expect their data to be deleted ([WebKit blog](https://webkit.org/blog/10218/full-third-party-cookie-blocking-and-more/)). Whether that seven-day rule still applies to ordinary (non-installed) sites on current iPhones is **unconfirmed**: WebKit's 2023 storage article does not mention it ([WebKit storage update](https://webkit.org/blog/14403/updates-to-storage-policy/)). Private mode, uninstalling, switching phones and "storage cleaner" apps are very likely causes too, but the notes found no authoritative source for cleaner apps or uninstall behaviour, so treat those as likely, not proven.

**Item: "last backup" indicator, reminder and storage protection. Verdict: DO NOW.** Ask the browser to mark the data "persistent" (`navigator.storage.persist()`) after the first saved transaction, show the answer in Settings, show "last backup: N days ago" on a main screen, and handle "storage full" and two-tabs-open overwrites. Chrome grants persistence silently for engaged or installed sites and Safari decides by its own signals, such as being opened as a Home Screen app ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/persistent-storage); [WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/14403/updates-to-storage-policy/)). Evidence strength: Strong for how storage behaves, Weak for whether reminders change behaviour. The one study found measured intentions, not actions, and reminders did not raise intent to set up automatic backups ([Frik et al., WEIS 2018](https://blues.cs.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/WEIS_2018_paper_42.pdf)), so the plan is to make backing up one tap rather than rely on nagging. Cost: about 2 to 4 days (team estimate). Risks: persistence is not a guarantee against the user clearing data; the multi-tab guard also protects financial correctness, which is why it belongs in this step. Position: 1.

**Item: better backup files and restore. Verdict: DO NOW (right after the item above).** Dated file names, a record count and a checksum so a damaged file is detected, a preview before restoring (counts, date range, totals), and an automatic safety copy of current data before any restore. These are the research team's good-practice checklist, not something verified in other apps (the notes found no evidence about how Obsidian, Standard Notes or others do it; only Actual Budget was confirmed to offer a portable export alongside local storage ([Actual Budget docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/getting-started/sync/))). Evidence: Weak (design reasoning), but the risk is low and the permanent storage key `expense_ledger_v5` stays untouched. Cost: 3 to 5 days including a restore round-trip test. Risk: bugs in restore are themselves a data-loss path, so test failure cases such as truncated files. Position: 2.

**Item: "Send backup to myself" through the share menu. Verdict: DO NOW, but test on real phones first.** The Web Share API can hand a file to other apps, needs HTTPS and a tap, and must be feature-checked with `canShare`; it is not available everywhere (MDN marks it "limited availability") ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/web-share); [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Navigator/canShare)). Azerbaijan fit looks good because WhatsApp and Telegram are widely used (the only measure found counts websites embedding them, not users: WhatsApp 59.9% and Telegram 45% of Azerbaijani sites ([WMTips](https://www.wmtips.com/technologies/instant-messaging/country/az/))). Unknown: whether a `.json` file is accepted by each share target; a `.txt` or `.csv` fallback may be needed. Always keep a plain download button as a fallback. Cost: 2 to 4 days. Risk: depends on the phone; backups are manual. Position: 3.

**Item: emailing history automatically. Verdict: DO NOT DO.** A "type your email, receive everything" feature needs a server function with a secret key (or a public-key service that anyone can abuse), an open endpoint that could be used to send mail to strangers, and sending full financial data to a processor. The free options are weak: EmailJS offers 200 requests a month with a 50 KB cap and conflicting statements about attachments ([EmailJS](https://www.emailjs.com/docs/faq/can-i-use-emailjs-for-free/)); Web3Forms attachments are paid and submissions go to the site owner, not an arbitrary address ([Web3Forms](https://docs.web3forms.com/getting-started/pro-features/file-attachments)); Resend allows 100 emails a day and needs a secret key kept on a server ([Resend](https://resend.com/pricing.md)); Brevo's free plan allows 300 a day, with attachment rules unverified ([Brevo](https://www.brevo.com/features/email-api/)); the old free Cloudflare plus MailChannels route was shut down in 2024 ([MailChannels notice](https://support.mailchannels.com/hc/en-us/articles/26814255454093-End-of-Life-Notice-Cloudflare-Workers)). A `mailto:` link cannot carry the file and breaks at roughly 2,000 characters, and one report says Cyrillic text hit trouble near 500 ([SitePoint](https://www.sitepoint.com/community/t/mailto-body-length/5156)). Vercel's free Hobby plan is for non-commercial use only ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby-plan)), which matters if the product is ever sold. Evidence: Medium to Strong on the limits; the pricing pages themselves were not all read.

**Later options.** Encrypting backups with a passphrase (DO LATER, default off, with a big warning that a forgotten passphrase means permanent loss; the encryption settings come from the team's background knowledge and need checking against security guidance before building; 3 to 5 days). A Google Drive hidden-folder backup is technically possible with a low-sensitivity permission ([Google](https://developers.google.com/drive/api/guides/about-auth)) but brings a Google project, consent screen, privacy policy and verification, so DO LATER only if users ask. File System Access auto-saving works only on Chromium (not Safari or Firefox) ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_window_showsavefilepicker)), so it is an optional bonus. Periodic background sync is Chromium-only for installed apps and cannot be relied on, especially on iPhone ([Chrome developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/periodic-background-sync)), so any "automatic" backup must really be "prompt on open". QR backup holds too little for a full ledger (the 3 KB figure is unverified) and an extra IndexedDB copy helps only against rare write failures because clearing site data wipes both: DO NOT DO as a headline feature.

**Item: teaching users what causes data loss and how to install. Verdict: DO NOW (copy and small screens, 2 to 3 days).** Show short, timed messages: after the first transaction ("saved on this device only"), after about a week or N entries ("make your first backup"), a plain "what can erase your data" card in Settings (clearing browser data, private window, uninstalling, new phone, a full phone), and an explicit warning before any reset. Teaching installation is part of the same message because installed apps are treated better by Safari and Chrome. On Chromium, capture the install event, hold it, and show your own "Install" button after the first saved transaction, once and dismissible; Safari and Firefox do not support that event ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Progressive_web_apps/How_to/Trigger_install_prompt)). On iPhone show a three-step picture (Share, Add to Home Screen, Add). iOS 26 now opens any site added to the Home Screen as a web app by default, with an "Open as Web App" toggle ([heise](https://heise.de/-10749652)), so the steps differ by iOS version. Inside in-app browsers (for example opened from a social app), installing is generally unavailable, so show "Open in Safari/Chrome" instead; that approach is the team's own idea and unsourced. Samsung Internet and exact detection methods were not researched. Position: 4, together with the backup items.

## The "one-off" label is misleading and the cheapest honest fix

**Confirmed in this project's code (per the notes, not re-checked by this report):** the pill is not something the user sets. It is guessed per merchant, and "one-off" is the default for any merchant the detector does not classify as recurring (fewer than 3 transactions or irregular gaps), so a shop visited 20 times irregularly is labelled "one-off". It is a 9 px badge, and the current strings are "one-off" / "birdəfəlik" / "разовый". **Verdict: DO NOW.** Show a badge only when there is positive evidence, such as "Repeats monthly" or "Repeats weekly", and show nothing otherwise; if a second state is wanted, use "Occasional" or "Seen once", and reserve "One-time" for entries the user marks that way. Raise the text to at least 12 px and add a short "Based on how often you paid this place" explanation. The wording options in the notes include az and ru drafts, which need native review. Evidence: Strong for the problem (it is in the code), Weak for the best wording (no competitor labelling was found). Cost: under 1 day. Risk: display-only, no stored data changes. Position: 5, because it costs almost nothing and removes a false statement.

## Everyday screens: what is cheap, what is only a judgment call

**Home: latest transactions. Verdict: DO NOW.** Recent-activity lists are the common pattern: Beacon Budget combines a free-to-spend figure, recent activity and upcoming expenses ([Beacon Budget](https://dasbudget.userjot.com/updates/p/a-new-home-screen-budget-analysis----beacon-budget-v0-16-0)), PocketGuard offers widgets for latest transactions, upcoming bills and leftover money ([PocketGuard](https://pocketguard.com/news/stay-on-track-with-new-home-screen-widgets/)), and a Monzo design review praises seeing spent versus left at the top ([Yahoo](https://tech.yahoo.com/apps/articles/why-monzos-ui-smashes-competition-090000769.html)). That is vendor and press material, so evidence is Medium for "this is normal" and nothing for "users in Azerbaijan want it first". Suggested layout: one main number, then the last 5 transactions grouped by day with "See all", then "Coming up" only if data exists. Cost: 1 to 2 days. Risk: low, but loan payments and transfers must be labelled so they are not read as spending. Position: 6.

**All-time overview at the top of Analytics. Verdict: DO LATER, after a correctness test plan.** The notes found no sources at all, so this is pure design reasoning. The real danger is confusion between numbers that change with the month selector and numbers that do not, and incorrect totals if transfers, loans and debts are included. If built: label it "All time since [first record date]", keep it visually separate from the period charts, exclude transfers, show other currencies separately, and verify with independently calculated totals. Cost: 2 to 3 days plus tests. Position: 9.

**Modernising the 6-month chart. Verdict: DO LATER, in a modest form.** For ordered monthly data, vertical bars are fine if they start at zero ([Datawrapper](https://www.datawrapper.de/academy/why-our-column-and-bar-charts-start-at-zero)); truncated bars exaggerate differences ([Correll et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.02035v2)). Accessibility guidance says to offer a data table alongside the chart and meaningful descriptions ([Highcharts](https://www.highcharts.com/docs/accessibility/tables)). Suggested changes (design opinion, not tested): highlight the selected month and mute others, mark the current month as "so far" so it is not compared with full months, add a labelled average line (complete months only), show the value only for the tapped bar, and let a tap filter the list below. Do not add 3D, gradients or labels on every bar. Cost: 3 to 5 days. Risk: partial-month and average calculations need tests. Position: 10.

**Item: the "+" button with a type chooser. Verdict: DO NOW as a small, measured change, with Expense first.** Material-style guidance supports one bottom-right action and a short list of 3 to 6 related actions, and a modal bottom sheet is a documented pattern for listing actions ([MUI](https://v5-0-6.mui.com/components/speed-dial/); [Material bottom sheets](https://m1.material.io/components/bottom-sheets.html)). No evidence was found that a chooser-first flow is faster or slower than a form with a type switch, and a critical paper exists about floating action buttons whose argument was not read ([Crossref record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3390%2FFI11070148)). The owner's order (expense, income, loan, debt) is sensible, with plain one-line helpers such as "Money you spent". Show the chosen type as a changeable chip so a wrong tap costs one tap, and count taps to save an expense before and after. Cost: 2 to 3 days. Risk: slows the most common action (adding an expense) by one tap. Position: 7. If the measurement shows a slowdown, make a tap on "+" open Expense directly and put the chooser behind a long press or a second control.

**Item: number pad (smaller, hideable, animated). Verdict: DO NOW for hide/shrink and the system-keyboard fallback; DO LATER for animation polish.** Browsers already give a decimal keypad with `inputmode="decimal"`, so a custom pad is a product choice, not a necessity ([WebKit bug](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=200226); [Brad Frost](https://bradfrost.com/blog/post/better-numerical-inputs-for-mobile-forms/)). The notes found no data proving a custom pad is faster, so do not claim it. Practical rules from the notes: keys must be real buttons with labels, accept both comma and period (az and ru users commonly type commas, and a comma bug already occurred in this project in an earlier run), keep the pad to about 40% of the screen height (team estimate), let it collapse so the note, date and category fields stay reachable, and offer "use system keyboard" for people who prefer it or use screen readers. Key size targets: Apple 44 points, Material 48 dp, with the WCAG minimum at 24 px ([testparty](https://testparty.ai/blog/wcag-target-size-guide)). Animation should respect the reduce-motion setting ([W3C](https://w3c.github.io/wcag/understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)); keep key feedback near 100 ms (secondhand attribution to NN/g). Vibration works on Android Chrome only and is unsupported on iOS Safari per caniuse ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_navigator_vibrate)); do not rely on it. Unverified and must be tested on devices: behaviour of the pad next to the native keyboard, VoiceOver and TalkBack use, and iPhone installed-app behaviour. Cost: 3 to 5 days. Position: 8.

## Look and feel: icons, palette, dark theme, themes

**Icons, logo, app icon. Verdict: DO NOW (about 1 day for icons, half a day for the app icon).** Use a permissively licensed icon library imported one icon at a time: Lucide (ISC licence, only imported icons are bundled ([Lucide](https://lucide.dev/guide/packages/lucide-react))), Tabler (MIT ([Tabler](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tabler/tabler-icons/main/LICENSE))) or Phosphor (MIT ([Phosphor](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/phosphor-icons/homepage/master/LICENSE))). Draw only the logo and a few finance-specific icons yourself, matching the library's grid and stroke. Bundle size was not measured, so check it after the build. For the app icon, Chrome needs 192 and 512 pixel icons; the maskable version must keep its artwork inside the central circle (40% radius) on an opaque background, and should be a separate entry rather than "any maskable" ([web.dev maskable](https://web.dev/articles/maskable-icon); [web.dev manifest](https://web.dev/articles/add-manifest)). iPhone wants an opaque 180 px touch icon; those details come from blogs, not Apple documents, so the Apple guidelines should be read before finalising ([Netguru](https://www.netguru.com/codestories/pwa-ios)). Skip iPhone splash images for now. Evidence: Strong on licences and icon rules, Weak on the effort numbers. Risk: mixed icon styles look inconsistent. Position: 11.

**Palette. Verdict: DO NOW, together with dark theme.** Define named colours for roles (surface, text, accent, income, expense, warning) and test their contrast: 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text and for chart or control shapes ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode-users-issues); [W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)). No verified colour-blind-safe set exceeds about nine colours (Paul Tol "muted" has nine ([khroma](https://www.stats.bris.ac.uk/R/web/packages/khroma/vignettes/tol.html))), so 12 to 15 categories must also be told apart by icon and label, never colour alone. Do not rely on red versus green for expense and income. For Azerbaijan, colour preferences and flag-colour norms are unknown: the notes found no evidence, so look at three or four local bank apps and ask a few users instead of assuming.

**Dark theme. Verdict: DO NOW as "follow the phone's setting" (1 to 3 days including checking every screen, team estimate).** In NN/g's survey of 115 mobile users about a third preferred dark, a third light and a third mixed, they think of dark mode as a phone setting, and the authors recommend mirroring the device setting by default with an optional in-app switch ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode-users-issues)). Not having it is rarely a reason to leave, but peers have it: Monzo ([Monzo](https://monzo.com/help/app-help/switch-on-dark-mode-web/)), YNAB ([YNAB](https://www.ynab.com/the-new-design-is-rad-ynab-theme-updates)) and, according to an app-store listing and one user comment (not official), Birbank in May 2026 ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/birbank/1293207342)). Ignore the widely quoted "75% prefer dark" numbers; the notes could not trace them. Technically, `color-scheme` and separate theme colours per scheme avoid a white flash at launch ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/color-scheme)). Risks: hard-coded colours in charts and SVG, contrast regressions, and longer Azerbaijani and Russian text. Offer a three-way setting (system, light, dark) only if the extra few hours are acceptable. Position: 12. Strength of recommendation: Medium; it is catch-up parity, not a growth lever.

**Fully different themes. Verdict: DO NOT DO.** No rigorous evidence was found that cosmetic or paid themes improve retention or revenue in finance apps; the engagement figures found came from blogs with secondhand attribution ([EMB](https://blog.emb.global/ultimate-guide-to-app-customization)). YNAB and Monzo ship several themes for accessibility and brand reasons, not proven retention ([Monzo high-contrast](https://community.monzo.com/t/now-in-labs-high-contrast-themes/152056)). Each extra theme multiplies checking work across screens and three languages (the effort figure of 0.5 to 1 day per theme plus ongoing checks is an unsourced estimate). The one exception worth revisiting later is a high-contrast variant for accessibility.

## Meaning, safety and onboarding features that need extra care

**Recurring payments with an optional end date and countdown. Verdict: DO NOW (high value, 3 to 5 days, team estimate).** Firefly III supports a last-date or a number-of-repetitions cap, used one at a time ([Firefly III model](https://docs.rs/crate/firefly-iii/latest/source/src/models/recurrence_properties.rs)), and Actual Budget schedules can recur indefinitely, end on a date, or happen once ([Actual docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/schedules)). Evidence on YNAB, Monarch, Wallet, Spendee and others was not retrieved. Recommended design: store start date plus either an end date (counted inclusive, "until December") or a number of payments, and compute "3 of 12 paid, 3 left, last payment 15 Mar 2027" instead of storing it. Remember the intended day (for example the 31st) and adjust per month, otherwise it drifts to the 28th for good; Actual's docs describe the opposite problem, where choosing the 31st skips months ([Actual docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/schedules)). Never use JavaScript's date parsing on date-only strings (it shifts by a day in some time zones) and test with several time zones. Define what pause, skip, and edit mean for already-posted payments before coding (the notes recommend that posted history never changes). Linking to loans and debts is a second step. Russian needs three plural forms ("1 платёж, 2-4 платежа, 5+ платежей"). Position: 13.

**Sample or demo persona datasets. Verdict: DO NOW in a safe form, about 3 to 5 days.** Useful for first launch, tests and demos. Safety rules: a visible "Sample data" banner, every record flagged, never mixed with real data silently (offer only on an empty ledger or in a separate store so `expense_ledger_v5` is untouched until the user confirms), one tap to remove, and "DEMO_" in exported file names. Use fixed seed values, whole qepik amounts and a fixed date so tests are repeatable. Persona amounts can be anchored to 2026 figures from the press citing official bodies: minimum wage AZN 400 ([Report.az](https://report.az/en/finance/minimum-wage-in-azerbaijan-may-remain-at-azn400-in-2026)), average monthly salary about AZN 1,177 for January to July 2026 ([Report.az](https://report.az/en/finance/average-monthly-salary-in-azerbaijan-rises-7)), average pension about AZN 594 ([Report.az](https://report.az/en/finance/sspf-average-monthly-pension-in-azerbaijan-rises-to-azn543)), metro and bus fare AZN 0.60 ([Mincom](https://mincom.gov.az/en/media-en/news/approval-of-new-fares-for-metro-and-bus-routes-with-cashless-payment-systems-and-modern-standards)). Rent, gas and groceries have no authoritative source (one-bedroom Baku rent estimates range from about AZN 540 to 900 from aggregators ([getcostidea](https://getcostidea.com/?p=6387))), so label them illustrative. Suggested personas: student, salaried family with an instalment loan, freelancer with uneven income, pensioner, small shop owner (tax rules need local checking). Evidence about how other apps handle demo mode was not retrieved. Position: 14.

**Tips that teach better results. Verdict: DO LATER, in a small version (2 to 3 days for the rule engine plus native review of text).** The evidence is cautionary. A study of personalised overspending messages in a Canadian bank app found lasting lower spending but also fewer later logins ([NYU Stern](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/experience-stern/news/phd-candidate-sung-lee-s-research-overspending-messages-and-personal-finance-management-referenced)), and a summary says there is no firm evidence that budgeting apps improve financial wellbeing ([Behavioral Economics](https://behavioraleconomics.com/the-budgeting-app-trap-when-spending-information-backfires)). So: only data-triggered tips, one at a time, about two a week at most (that cap is judgment, not sourced), always with "Got it", "Not now" and "Don't show these", no guilt wording, no score (already decided), and a "Tips" list where all can be turned off. The most useful ones protect data quality: add income, set a limit for your biggest category, make a backup, sort uncategorised entries, and "Is 1200.00 correct?" for a likely typo. Effectiveness cannot be measured without analytics, so test with 5 to 10 real users. Position: 16.

**Family feature. Verdict: DO LATER, phase 1 only (a "who paid" tag, 2 to 4 days); DO NOT DO real-time sharing now.** US surveys say most couples keep at least some money separate (Bankrate February 2026: about 38% fully combine, 26% fully separate; earlier releases differ) ([Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/press-releases/most-couples-keep-at-least-some-of-their-money-separate/)), and tools for shared finances are documented tools of abuse and control ([NNEDV](https://www.techsafety.org/financial-abuse-and-technology); [USENIX 2023](https://www.usenix.net/system/files/usenixsecurity23-bellini.pdf)). There is no Azerbaijani evidence on how families manage money, so the idea that extended-family spending matters more than couples is a hypothesis; interview 5 to 10 people before building more. Phase 1: an optional member tag, filter and per-member totals, with a plain warning that anyone using the device sees everything. Phase 2, only if people use phase 1: each member exports a file and one person imports it with duplicate detection and a preview (4 to 7 days, team estimate). Separate profiles on one device are a data-safety risk for the permanent storage key and give no real privacy: avoid. Live syncing between phones (Yjs, Automerge) and paid server sync are 3-plus-week rewrites with ongoing costs; do not start without proven demand ([Yjs](https://docs.yjs.dev)). Position: 17.

**Detailed privacy policy. Verdict: DO NOW for a short, honest plain-language policy; DO NOT claim legal compliance until a local lawyer reviews it.** The model to follow is short: Actual Budget's policy has six headings and says data stays on the device ([Actual Budget](https://actualbudget.org/docs/privacy-policy)). Core statement the team can honestly make if the app never transmits finance data: "We do not receive, store or have access to your financial data." The only confirmed third party touching users is the host: Vercel records IP addresses in logs, acts as a processor for customers' end-user data, and publishes no specific retention period ([Vercel privacy notice](https://vercel.com/legal/privacy-policy)). The policy must say plainly what the app does not protect: no encryption inside the browser, anyone who can open the phone or browser profile can see the data, and backup files are not encrypted unless that is added. Before publishing, check the real app for third-party requests (fonts, analytics, error reporting) in the browser's network tab; the notes say fonts are self-hosted per the owner but this was not verified. Offer az, ru and en with equal prominence, with a short summary at the top. Google Play and Apple treat data processed only on the device as not "collected", but still require a privacy-policy link if the app is ever wrapped and listed ([Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469?hl=en); [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)). Any future email or cloud backup would change the policy and the legal position, so it must be reviewed first. Cost: 1 to 2 days to draft, plus the lawyer's time. Position: 15 (draft early; the lawyer step runs in parallel).

**First-launch experience. Verdict: DO NOW (3 to 5 days with translations).** The owner's rule of explicit az, ru and en choices on the first screen fits the evidence. NN/g advises against promotional tours and prefers learning in context with a visible Skip ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-app-onboarding/)). Vendor benchmarks (SaaS, not finance apps) say user-started tours complete far more than auto-started ones (69% versus 23%) and short tours far more than long ones ([Produktly](https://produktly.com/research/saas-onboarding-benchmarks-2026)); treat these numbers as direction only. Proposed flow: language choice, then an empty screen with one action ("Add your first expense"), an optional three-card "How it works" with Skip, a small checklist (add account, add an expense, make a backup), an offer of clearly labelled sample data, and the install and backup messages described above at the right moments. No az or ru onboarding research or finance-app tour data was found. Position: 6 (alongside the Home screen, since both shape the first session).

## Recommended order and roadmap

The order puts data safety and false statements first because they carry the highest risk; next come cheap fixes that every user sees; then bigger features that need design and tests. Days are the research team's rough estimates and exclude native-speaker review of Azerbaijani and Russian text.

| Order | Item | Verdict | Cost | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Backup health: last-backup indicator, `persist()`, storage-full and multi-tab protection | DO NOW | 2-4 days | Cannot stop users clearing data |
| 2 | Backup file v2: dated name, checksum, restore preview, safety copy before restore, round-trip test | DO NOW | 3-5 days | Restore bugs lose data; test failure paths |
| 3 | "Send backup to myself" via share menu with download fallback | DO NOW, after real-phone test | 2-4 days | File types and support vary by phone |
| 4 | Data-loss and install teaching (timed messages, Settings card, install button, iPhone steps) | DO NOW | 2-3 days | Annoying if too frequent |
| 5 | Fix "one-off" label | DO NOW | under 1 day | Display only |
| 6 | Home: latest 5 transactions; first-launch flow | DO NOW | 1-2 and 3-5 days | Loans shown as spending |
| 7 | "+" type chooser (measure taps) | DO NOW | 2-3 days | Slows adding an expense |
| 8 | Number pad: collapsible, smaller, system-keyboard fallback | DO NOW (animation later) | 3-5 days | Keyboard quirks, accessibility |

Practical grouping: Release A is items 1 to 5 (trust and safety). Release B is items 6 to 8 and 11 to 12 (everyday experience and look). Release C is items 13 and 14 (new capability and onboarding material). Items 15 to 19 follow user feedback. The privacy policy draft should start in parallel with Release A because the lawyer's turnaround is outside our control. One caution on sequencing: items 3, 4 and 12 touch the first-launch and settings screens, so build them with the first-launch design in mind to avoid redoing screens.

## What we could not verify and what to check next

These are gaps in the research, not findings. Treat every claim in them as unproven.

Law and privacy. The text of Azerbaijan's Law on Personal Data (No. 998-IIIQ) was never read; the notes rely on secondary summaries that disagree on whether written consent is always required and on which authority regulates it ([DataGuidance](https://www.dataguidance.com/notes/azerbaijan-data-protection-overview)). **A local lawyer must confirm** whether a local-only app needs any registration, whether hosting IP logs make the developer an "operator", whether there is a minimum age, and what changes if backup or sync is ever added. Whether Russian data-localisation rules reach Russian-speaking users outside Russia is disputed between two sources ([Kramer Levin](https://kramerlevin.com/en/perspectives-search/increased-fines-for-violations-of-russian-localization-law.html) versus [Presencis](https://cdn.presencis.com/regulations/ru-152fz/applicability/)); ask the lawyer. Vercel's log retention period is unpublished; ask Vercel or read its data agreement. Nothing here is legal advice.

Real-device tests. None of the following has been tested on a phone: Web Share with a file on Android Chrome, iPhone Safari and the installed app (and whether `.json` is accepted by Telegram, WhatsApp, Gmail and Drive); the install button on Chrome and the instructions on iPhone (including iOS 26 and the "Open as Web App" toggle); the custom number pad with and without the native keyboard, VoiceOver and TalkBack; vibration on Android; whether data survives a week of inactivity on an iPhone for installed and non-installed use; and restoring a backup onto a different phone.

Safari storage rules. The seven-day deletion rule is documented in 2020 sources and by MDN, but WebKit's 2023 storage update does not mention it, so whether it still applies today is unconfirmed. Behaviour of storage-cleaner apps and of uninstalling the installed app is unsourced.

Competitor behaviour. The research could not retrieve how Monzo, Revolut, YNAB, Wallet, Spendee, Money Manager, Copilot, Monarch, Bluecoins or Cashew handle the "+" flow, number pad, recurring end dates, demo data, tips or backups, and the official local bank apps (ABB Mobile, Birbank) were not examined except for one unofficial note that Birbank added dark mode. Next step: the owner or a helper looks at these apps directly (screenshots or app-store videos), and we then fetch each help centre. Do not copy a competitor feature just because it exists.

Evidence from other disciplines. There is no direct test of custom versus system number pad speed, no study of chooser-first versus toggle add flows, no usability research on what Azerbaijani users want first on Home, and no evidence that colour themes help retention. The onboarding numbers (69% versus 23%) are vendor SaaS data. The 2018 backup-reminder study measured intentions only. The family and Bankrate figures are from the United States; **no Azerbaijani data on household money management** was found.

Pricing and technical limits. Official, current pricing and attachment limits for EmailJS, Brevo, Resend and Formspree were not all read, and Chrome version support figures for Web Share and file saving conflict between sources. Icon bundle size was not measured. The AES-GCM and PBKDF2 settings for encrypted backups come from background knowledge and need a security check. The 3 KB QR capacity is unverified.

Local numbers. Salary and pension figures come from press quoting the State Statistics Committee and the pension fund, not from official pages; rent, gas tariff, groceries and petrol have no authoritative source. Before using personas in public, check the State Statistics Committee bulletins and the energy regulator's tariff page.

Language. All Azerbaijani and Russian wording in the notes is a draft. Have a native speaker review every string, including Russian plural forms and the legal and privacy text.

## Open questions for the owner

1. Is the app staying free with no accounts for the foreseeable future? The answer decides whether Vercel's non-commercial free plan, a lawyer, and any later paid backup are relevant.
2. Are you comfortable with the share-menu backup ("Send to myself") as the answer to "email me my history", knowing it needs one tap from the user and cannot run in the background?
3. Which phones do your first users have (mostly iPhone or Android)? This decides which tests come first, because iPhone has the strictest storage and install rules.
4. Can you test on at least one iPhone and one Android phone, and can you find 5 to 10 people (students, parents, an older person) for short tests? We need real people to settle the "+" chooser, the number pad, the Home screen, tips and the family feature.
5. Should adding an expense stay a single tap, with the type chooser reached another way, if testing shows the chooser slows people down?
6. Do you know a lawyer or an organisation in Azerbaijan to review the privacy policy and the personal-data law questions, and when can they do it?
7. For the family feature, who do you picture sharing: a couple, parents and children, or extended family? This changes what phase 1 should be.
8. Is a native speaker available for Azerbaijani and Russian text review, ideally one person per language?
9. Are there colours, symbols or flag colours you want to use or avoid in the logo and palette? Would you like a short check of local bank apps before the palette is fixed?
10. Do you want demo personas to be visible to every new user at first launch, or only available from Settings?
11. Which should come first if time is short: Release A (data safety) or Release B (look and everyday screens)? The research recommends A.

## Conclusion

The research changes one assumption: the owner's most visible ideas (emailing history, many themes, a family sync) are the riskiest or least supported, while the unglamorous items (a backup health indicator, a restore with a safety copy, an honest label, an install and data-loss explanation) address the real ways users lose their ledger and cost little. The honest framing for a free, local-first app is not "automatic protection" but "make backing up one tap and make the risk visible", because no free mechanism can back up in the background on iPhone.

Two things matter beyond the roadmap. First, many UI recommendations here are plausible design reasoning, not evidence; the cheapest way to turn them into evidence is a handful of short tests with real users on real phones, which the plan should schedule rather than skip. Second, the legal position of a no-server app in Azerbaijan is unconfirmed, so any future feature that sends data off the device should wait for a local lawyer's answer.
