# Budgeting, saving and accounting frameworks for a rule-based personal-finance coach

All pages accessed 2026-10-30. Labels: PRIMARY (original/official), SECONDARY (vendor, media, explainer), INFERENCE (my reasoning, not from a source). Research was limited by search rate-limiting (about 15 calls); several items are flagged as gaps rather than filled from memory.

## 1. Frameworks: 50/30/20, pay-yourself-first, zero-based/YNAB, envelope, emergency fund, sinking funds, cash-flow forecasting

### Takeaway
50/30/20, zero-based budgeting and the 3-6 month emergency-fund rule are widely used heuristics with thin causal evidence; the rule of thumb is attributed to unnamed "experts", not to a study. Automatic or pre-committed saving has the best behavioural evidence (Save More Tomorrow), but even that is quasi-experimental. A coach should present these as adjustable guides, never as pass/fail standards.

### Cited Findings
- 50/30/20: after-tax income split 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% savings and debt repayment; from *All Your Worth* (Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi). Sources disagree on whether Warren originated or only popularised it, and give the year as 2005 or 2006. Percentages are described as adjustable (60/20/20 is a common variant). SECONDARY — [Acorns](https://www.acorns.com/money-basics/saving-and-budgeting/what-is-the-50-30-20-budget-rule-/), [InCharge](https://www.incharge.org/financial-literacy/budgeting-saving/50-30-20-rule/), [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/does-50-30-20-budgeting-rule-still-really-work)
- Housing is about one third of average US household spending (33.2% in 2023-24 BLS data), transportation 17.0%, food 12.9%; housing share ranges from 29.1% (St. Louis) to 40.0% (Miami). This shows a fixed 50% "needs" ceiling does not fit all places; US data only, nothing found for Azerbaijan. PRIMARY — [BLS regional release](https://www.bls.gov/regions/northeast/news-release/2026/consumerexpenditures_boston_20260106.htm)
- YNAB's four rules (Give Every Dollar a Job; Embrace Your True Expenses; Roll With the Punches; Age Your Money). "Give every dollar a job" = assign only money you currently have to categories until "Ready to Assign" is zero (zero-based). True expenses = set aside monthly for irregular costs (car repair, gifts). Roll with punches = move money between categories when you overspend. Age your money = pay this month's bills with money from earlier months. SECONDARY (search summary of YNAB pages) — [YNAB four rules](https://www.ynab.com/ynab-four-rules-budgeting). Note: that page as fetched on 2026-10-30 no longer lists "four rules" but reframes the method around "For Now / For Later / For Ease / For You / For Change"; "For Later" = small monthly sums for irregular expenses (sinking-fund logic) and "For Ease" = cover next month's bills in advance (age of money). PRIMARY (fetched page).
- Emergency fund rule of thumb: St. Louis Fed says "experts often recommend" 3-6 months of essential expenses; method = total monthly essentials x 3 (example $2,400 -> $7,200). The article cites no study for the 3-6 range; survey data show how many people hold savings, not whether 3 vs 6 months gives better outcomes. PRIMARY — [St. Louis Fed Page One Economics, Sept 2025](https://www.stlouisfed.org/publications/page-one-economics/2025/sep/when-unexpected-happens-be-ready-with-emergency-fund)
- Federal Reserve SHED 2025 (published 13 May 2026; fielded Oct 17-28, 2025; about 13,000 adults): 63% would cover a $400 expense with cash or equivalent (68% in 2021); among adults earning under $50,000, 4 in 10 could not cover a $100 emergency from savings alone. PRIMARY — [Federal Reserve SHED page](https://www.federalreserve.gov/consumerscommunities/shed.htm), [SHED 2025 summary](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-executive-summary.htm)
- SHED 2024 (as quoted by St. Louis Fed): 55% had set aside 3 months of expenses; 13% could not pay $400 by any means; 3-month cushion by age 36% (18-29), 50% (30-44), 54% (45-59), 72% (60+). PRIMARY — [St. Louis Fed](https://www.stlouisfed.org/publications/page-one-economics/2025/sep/when-unexpected-happens-be-ready-with-emergency-fund). Secondary sites mix up the 2024 and 2025 editions; cite the Fed itself.
- Irregular income: Vanguard (Aug 2025) says required emergency fund depends heavily on income variability; suggests budgeting from a baseline spend and saving income above baseline in high months; keep liquid. Its 2025 survey (12,443 investors) associates $2,000 saved, and 3-6 months saved, with higher financial well-being (associations only). SECONDARY (search summary, page not fetched) — [Vanguard](https://investor.vanguard.com/investor-resources-education/article/understanding-income-volatility-and-emergency-saving)
- JPMorgan Chase Institute "Weathering Volatility" (2017): buffer to cover 90% of adverse income/spending swings about $4,800 for median-income households vs about $3,000 liquid savings held. "Weathering Volatility 2.0" (2013-2018 Chase checking data, over six million families): about six weeks of take-home income in liquid assets covers a simultaneous income dip and spending spike; 65% of families lacked it; spending volatility about 15% below income volatility. Chase customers, US only. PRIMARY (search summary) — [JPMorgan Chase Institute](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/corporate/institute/report-weathering-volatility.htm)
- Pay-yourself-first / automatic saving evidence: Save More Tomorrow raised average savings rate from 3.5% to 13.6% over 40 months at one mid-sized US manufacturer. A US Department of Labor CLEAR review rates the causal evidence low because participant/non-participant differences were not controlled. PRIMARY/SECONDARY — [Chicago Booth abstract](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/research/rustandy/social-impact-research/research-papers/2004/save-more-tomorrow-using-behavioral-economics-to-increase-employee-saving), [DOL CLEAR](https://clear.dol.gov/study/save-more-tomorrow%E2%84%A2-using-behavioral-economics-increase-employee-saving-thaler-benartzi-2004). Note: the mechanism is automation by an employer, which a manual-entry app cannot replicate.
- Does budgeting work? Mixed. St Andrews (Money Dashboard app data plus diaries): budgeting reduces spending, more ambitious budgets reduce most, effect persisted at 6 months for partial compliers. Clarity Money randomized field test (about 9,000 users): no significant spending difference (control $675.97 vs single budget $681.08 vs category budgets $673.25); budgeters spent 1.3-1.4x what they budgeted; engagement rose. SECONDARY — [St Andrews](https://news.st-andrews.ac.uk/archive/dont-abandon-your-budget-it-works-says-university-of-st-andrews-research/), [Advanced Hindsight case study](https://case-studies.advanced-hindsight.com/case-studies/GfCI1P8I/clarity-money-budgeting-experiment)
- Envelope method: I found no study that tests envelope budgeting directly. The nearest research (Gathergood and Weber, co-holding puzzle) links impulsive spending to holding both savings and costly credit, read as self-control management. SECONDARY — [Nottingham repository](https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/29811/)

### Arithmetic reference (standard definitions; formulas are INFERENCE-level restatements unless cited above)
- 50/30/20: needs% = needs / net income; wants% = wants / net income; save% = (savings + extra debt repayment) / net income. Targets 50/30/20.
- Zero-based: unassigned = income available - sum(assigned categories); target 0.
- Emergency months = liquid savings / average monthly essential spend (St. Louis Fed multiplies essentials by 3 for the target).
- Sinking fund monthly amount = (expected irregular bill) / (months until due). INFERENCE; YNAB "true expenses" is the same idea.
- Simple cash-flow forecast: projected balance(t) = opening balance + expected income - expected outflows, using known recurring items only. INFERENCE; no authoritative source retrieved.

### Inferences
- 50/30/20 fits badly where needs exceed 50% (low income, high-rent cities) because it will show a permanent "fail"; the coach should compute the user's own split and compare to their own history rather than to 50/30/20. A "needs" label also requires the user to classify, which is subjective.
- Zero-based and age-of-money presuppose regular income and that all spending is recorded; with irregular or informal income and cash-heavy households the "Ready to Assign" figure is unreliable. Use baseline-month or lowest-recent-months income instead.
- The 3-6 month figure has no evidential support beyond expert convention; present it as a range, scale to income variability (Vanguard, JPMC), and offer a small first milestone (about six weeks of income per JPMC, or a fixed small buffer) rather than a "failing" status.
- Because evidence on budgeting effect is mixed, the coach should aim to improve awareness and data quality rather than claim it will reduce spending.

### Gaps
- Original text of *All Your Worth* and a primary source for the 50/30/20 origin were not retrieved (search rate-limited).
- No primary source retrieved for CFPB wording on emergency funds; a search found only secondary attributions, so I do not attribute "3-6 months" to CFPB.
- No academic evidence retrieved for envelope budgeting, sinking funds, or cash-flow forecasting accuracy in households.
- No data on Azerbaijan household spending shares, savings behaviour or informal income.
- SHED 2025 three-month-savings percentage not found on the pages fetched.

## 2. Accounting practice transferable to personal finance

### Takeaway
Variance analysis, cost classification and ratio thinking transfer well, but accounting has no standard materiality percentage; thresholds are judgement-based. DTI limits come from lending practice and regulation (the 43% QM cap was replaced in 2020 by a price-based test) and are for borrowers, not a measure of household health.

### Cited Findings
- Budget variance: revenue above budget is favourable and cost above budget is adverse; favourable variances can also signal wrong standards. A flexible budget restates the budget at actual activity so volume effects separate from performance. No authoritative (ACCA/AAT) threshold found; examples seen are 5% or 6% of budget, or dual triggers (percentage and amount), and frequency and persistence matter as well as size. SECONDARY — [Spendesk](https://www.spendesk.com/blog/budget-variance-analysis/), [Agicap](https://agicap.com/en/article/budgetary-variance/), [PQ Magazine](https://pqmagazine.com/?p=7394)
- DTI definition: add up monthly debt payments and divide by gross monthly income (CFPB example $2,000 / $6,000 = 33%). CFPB page (reviewed Aug 2023) says limits vary by loan product and lender and does not state 36%/43%. PRIMARY — [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-debt-to-income-ratio-why-is-the-43-debt-to-income-ratio-important-en-1791/)
- 43% DTI cap in the 2013 CFPB General Qualified Mortgage rule; the CFPB's December 2020 final rule replaced it with a price (APR) limit while still requiring lenders to consider DTI or residual income. The 28/36 rule (housing under 28% of gross income, total debt under 36%) is a traditional lender guideline, not a CFPB requirement. PRIMARY/SECONDARY — [Federal Register, 29 Dec 2020](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2020-12-29/pdf/2020-27567.pdf), [Dallas Fed](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2020/0324.aspx), [CFPB QM explained](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/blog/qualified-mortgages-explained/). Effective date of the 2020 change was not confirmed. These are US rules; Azerbaijani bank practice not researched.
- Liquidity as "weeks of income in liquid assets" (JPMC six weeks) is the empirically grounded personal analogue of working-capital buffers. PRIMARY (summary) — [JPMorgan Chase Institute](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/corporate/institute/report-weathering-volatility.htm)

### Definitions the coach could use (INFERENCE: textbook-standard, no source retrieved this session)
- Savings rate = (income - spending) / income, over a full period, with transfers between own accounts excluded.
- Expense-to-income = spending / income. Run-rate = average over recent complete periods annualised or projected; use median or trimmed values to limit one-off distortion.
- Fixed / variable / discretionary: rent and loan payments are fixed; groceries and fuel vary; entertainment is discretionary. Classification is by user category, so ask the user to tag, never guess.
- Cash-flow vs income-statement view: the app records cash movements; accrual-style items (annual insurance paid once) distort a single month, so compare on a rolling 3-month or 12-month view and spread irregular bills (sinking-fund view).
- Liquidity ratio = liquid balances / average monthly essential spend (months of cover).
- Debt service ratio = monthly debt payments / monthly net (or gross; state which) income.

### Inferences
- Use a dual materiality trigger (for example both a percentage of the category's budget and an absolute amount) so small categories do not generate noise; exact values must be tuned and are not sourced.
- DTI/debt-service thresholds should be shown as "lenders often look at" context, never as a verdict; the 43% cap no longer exists as a QM limit in the US, and the app has no data on rate, tenure or hidden loans.

### Gaps
- No CIMA/ACCA/AICPA primary text on variance materiality retrieved.
- No source retrieved for non-mortgage debt-service guidance, or for Azerbaijani lending norms.
- No source retrieved for "liquidity ratio" thresholds for households.

## 3. Meaningful metrics from typed entries only, and data-quality gates

### Takeaway
With manual entry, completeness is the main threat: any ratio is a lower bound on spending and an upper bound on savings. The coach should check coverage before speaking and word results as "from what you have recorded".

### Cited Findings
- Self-reported or manually tracked spending: I found no source quantifying under-reporting in expense diaries; the strongest budgeting studies used account-linked transaction data, not self-report. SECONDARY — [St Andrews](https://news.st-andrews.ac.uk/archive/dont-abandon-your-budget-it-works-says-university-of-st-andrews-research/), [Advanced Hindsight](https://case-studies.advanced-hindsight.com/case-studies/GfCI1P8I/clarity-money-budgeting-experiment)
- Budgeters in a field experiment spent 1.3-1.4x their budget, so budgets are aspirations rather than forecasts. SECONDARY — same Advanced Hindsight source.

### Inferences (all INFERENCE; candidate gates for implementation, thresholds to be tuned)
- Minimum history: do not state savings rate, run-rate or months-of-cover with fewer than about 14-28 days of entries or fewer than one complete calendar month; label estimates "early".
- No income recorded in the period: suppress savings rate and expense-to-income; offer to add income.
- Future-dated entries: exclude from "so far" totals and tell the user how many were excluded.
- Duplicates: same date, amount, category and account within a short window; flag and ask rather than auto-merge.
- Recording gaps: if the number of days with any entry in the period is low versus the user's normal logging cadence, or cash spending is rarely typed, say totals may be incomplete.
- Transfers between own accounts and loan principal must not count as spending or income.
- Currency: do not mix currencies in one ratio unless a rate is user-entered.
- Stale data: if the last entry is older than a set number of days, suppress "this month" claims.

### Gaps
- No empirical figure for how much cash spending manual-entry users omit.

## 4. Books and authoritative sources for the team

### Takeaway
Most popular books are opinion plus anecdote; the evidence-based layer is survey and administrative data (Fed SHED, JPMC Institute, BLS) and behavioural-economics studies. I could not verify book contents through search this session, so book summaries below are INFERENCE from background knowledge and should be checked against the books before being quoted.

### Cited Findings
- Authoritative data sources used above: Federal Reserve SHED (annual, since 2013 per the 13th survey in 2025) — [Fed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/consumerscommunities/shed.htm); BLS Consumer Expenditure — [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/regions/northeast/news-release/2026/consumerexpenditures_boston_20260106.htm); JPMorgan Chase Institute — [JPMC](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/corporate/institute/report-weathering-volatility.htm); CFPB Ask CFPB DTI page — [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-debt-to-income-ratio-why-is-the-43-debt-to-income-ratio-important-en-1791/); *All Your Worth* for 50/30/20 — see section 1; Thaler and Benartzi Save More Tomorrow — see section 1.

### Inferences (background knowledge, not verified in this session; no URL)
- *Your Money or Your Life* (Dominguez and Robin): track every cent, compute life energy cost of spending; opinion/method, no controlled evidence.
- *The Simple Path to Wealth* (JL Collins): low-cost index investing; investing opinion, outside a budgeting coach's scope.
- *You Need a Budget* (Jesse Mecham): YNAB method, vendor-authored; no independent trial found.
- *I Will Teach You to Be Rich* (Ramit Sethi): automation and "conscious spending plan"; opinion.
- *The Total Money Makeover* (Dave Ramsey): baby steps, debt snowball; opinion, contested versus highest-interest-first.
- *Beyond Budgeting* (Hope and Fraser): corporate management model replacing fixed annual budgets with rolling forecasts; supports rolling-baseline rather than fixed-target coaching. Accounting textbooks (CIMA/ACCA management accounting) cover variance analysis.
- Evidence-based vs opinion split: evidence-based = SHED, JPMC, BLS, behavioural studies on automation; opinion = percentage rules, baby steps, FIRE-type books.

- Book summaries could not be verified; not checked against any source.
- No Azerbaijani or Russian-language personal-finance sources researched.

## Implications for a coach rule (candidate rules; thresholds are INFERENCE and need tuning/user testing)

Global gate for all rules: at least one complete calendar month or 28 days of entries; no future-dated or flagged-duplicate entries in the window; else stay silent or show a data-quality note. Wording always "from what you have recorded".

1. Savings rate
   - Trigger: monthly close or user opens Insights. Formula: (income - spending) / income, own-account transfers excluded. Minimum: one full month, income recorded, 2+ weeks with entries. Message: "From your entries, you kept about X% of income this month." Silence: no income, income recorded in a different month than the spending, or negative months shown as "spent more than recorded income" without judgement. Compare to the user's own prior months, not a universal target.
2. Needs/wants/savings split (50/30/20 as optional lens)
   - Trigger: user opts in and has tagged categories as need/want. Formula: need% = needs/net income, etc. Minimum: 1 month, 80%+ of spending categorised. Message: "About X% of your income went to needs, Y% to wants, Z% to saving; 50/30/20 is a common starting guide, not a rule." Silence: untagged categories, needs over 50% for 3 months (switch to "your needs are your main cost" instead of a fail).
3. Category over typical level (variance)
   - Trigger: category spend exceeds its budget, or its own 3-month median, by both a percentage (for example 20%) and an absolute amount. Formula: variance = actual - budget (adverse if cost above). Minimum: 3 prior months or a set budget. Message: "Groceries are X above your usual for this point in the month." Silence: month under 7 days old, irregular category (use sinking-fund rule), favourable variances shown only as mild positive notes.
4. Pace check mid-month
   - Trigger: day 10-25. Formula: projected month total = spend so far / days elapsed x days in month, compared with budget or 3-month median. Minimum: 10 days of data and 3 prior months. Message: "At this pace you would reach about X by month end, against Y planned." Silence: large one-off already in total (offer to exclude), fewer than 10 days.
5. Months of cover
   - Trigger: liquid account balances known. Formula: liquid balance / average monthly essential spend over last 3 complete months. Minimum: 3 months of entries and user-confirmed liquid accounts. Message: "Your liquid savings cover about N months of your recorded essentials; 3 to 6 months is a common aim." Silence: no accounts marked liquid, essentials untagged, no first-milestone failure framing; show "about six weeks" first step (JPMC) when under 1.5 months. Widen suggested range when income is irregular.
6. Irregular income buffer
   - Trigger: coefficient of variation of monthly income above a set level over 6 months. Formula: baseline spend = median monthly essentials; excess income in high months above baseline to be set aside. Minimum: 6 months with income. Message: "Your income varies; setting aside income above your usual spending in good months can cover the lean ones." Silence: under 6 months or one-off income (bonus/refund) not marked.
7. Sinking fund suggestion
   - Trigger: a category or tag with an annual/irregular bill recorded (insurance, school fees, holiday) and a known next due date. Formula: amount / months remaining. Minimum: one prior occurrence or user-entered due date. Message: "To cover X due in N months, setting aside about Y a month would avoid a spike." Silence: no due date, under 1 month left (state shortfall instead).
8. Upcoming cash shortfall (simple forecast)
   - Trigger: user-entered recurring items and known due dates. Formula: projected balance = balance + expected income - recurring outflows, day by day for 30 days. Minimum: recurring items confirmed by user, balance updated within 7 days. Message: "Based on your recurring items, your balance may be below zero around [date]." Silence: balance stale, income timing uncertain, cash-heavy accounts where balance is not tracked.
9. Fixed-cost share
   - Trigger: monthly review. Formula: fixed (tagged) / income. Minimum: 2 months, fixed tags set. Message: "Fixed costs took X% of your income, which leaves less room to adjust quickly." Silence: no tags, no income; no threshold shown beyond the user's own trend.
10. Debt payments share
    - Trigger: user records debt payments with a debt tag. Formula: monthly debt payments / gross (or stated net) monthly income, state which. Minimum: 2 months income and debt tags. Message: "Debt payments are about X% of your income; lenders often look at this ratio." Silence: debts not fully recorded (say so), never state a pass/fail from 36% or 43% since those are US lender conventions.
11. Unassigned money (zero-based option)
    - Trigger: user opted into planning mode at month start. Formula: income expected - sum(planned categories). Minimum: planned income entered. Message: "X of your planned income has no purpose yet." Silence: irregular income users (plan from baseline), planning mode off.
12. Duplicate entry warning
    - Trigger: on save or review. Formula: same amount, date (+/- 1 day), account and similar note. Minimum: any. Message: "These two entries look the same; keep both or remove one?" Silence: recurring items legitimately repeating (rent, subscriptions) unless same day.
13. Recording gap notice
    - Trigger: more than N consecutive days without entries while the user has a normal logging cadence. Formula: days since last entry vs median gap. Minimum: 30 days of history. Message: "No entries for N days; summaries may be missing spending." Silence: new user, user paused tracking. Also suppress rules 1, 3-5, 9 while the notice is active, or attach "may be incomplete".
14. Future-dated entries notice
    - Trigger: any entry dated after today beyond a planned/scheduled flag. Formula: count of future entries. Message: "N entries are dated in the future and are left out of this month so far." Silence: entries explicitly marked scheduled.
15. Income missing
    - Trigger: month end with spending but no income recorded. Formula: income count = 0. Message: "No income is recorded this month, so savings and ratio figures are hidden." Silence: user chose spending-only mode.

Caveats for the whole set: heuristics are not individual advice; thresholds above are product-tuning placeholders, not sourced numbers; each rule should be unit-tested with independently computed expected values and reviewed in Azerbaijani, Russian and English; cash-heavy or informal-income users need the incompleteness wording by default.
