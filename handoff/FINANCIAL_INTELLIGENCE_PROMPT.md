# FINANCIAL INTELLIGENCE: ADVANCED QUALITY, NEW SYSTEMS AND PRODUCT EVOLUTION (owner's work order, saved 10 Nov 2026)
Saved as a condensed, requirement-complete copy for the build plan. Do not read it before the owner says to start this job. The owner pasted the full text in chat on 10 Nov; where this copy is ambiguous, ask the owner to paste it again.

## 1. Mission
Take the EXISTING Financial Intelligence (position, anticipating problems, better decisions, insight cards, verifiable calculations) to the next level: more accurate, reliable, useful, and investigate genuinely new systems. Do not rebuild what exists. Aim: a dependable Personal Financial OS that helps people understand the present, anticipate the future and decide better.

## 2. Constraints
The engineering master prompt and the Apple design reference stay authoritative. Before building: inspect the real repository and the existing Financial Intelligence, Coach, Smart Helper, dashboards, analytics and calculations; find what exists; avoid duplicate systems and competing calculation engines. Keep localStorage key expense_ledger_v5, data and migration compatibility; never silently modify financial records; no destructive migration; no push/merge/deploy; no removing code without dependency analysis.

## 3. Workstream A: advance the existing intelligence
A.1 Correctness audit of: financial position and available funds; runway; month-end forecast; forecasts with unpaid and recurring bills; shortfall dates before income; affordability; purchase impact on goals and obligations; extra debt repayments and payoff dates; avalanche vs snowball; unusual spending; subscription price changes; late or missing income. Test hard cases: irregular income and spending, multiple income sources, delayed or missing income, different due dates, partly paid and overdue bills, negative and very low and zero balances, missing or outdated history, month lengths, leap years and year boundaries, edited/deleted/duplicated transactions, changed recurring obligations, multiple currencies. Verify dates, signs, balances, assumptions, rounding, consistency.
A.2 Forecast honesty: separate actual figures, user expectations, confirmed obligations, system estimates, assumptions/inferred patterns. Expected income is never guaranteed. No false reassurance from incomplete data; consider freshness and completeness; say so when it cannot be calculated; no arbitrary confidence percentages.
A.3 Every important insight explains: what is happening, why it matters, which figures support it, which assumptions, what action could be considered (a shortfall warning gives date, amount, contributing obligations, income assumptions, risk-reducing actions; affordability explains consequences, not just yes/no). No generic advice.
A.4 Unusual spending and recurring detection: equivalent-period comparison, seasonal and infrequent purchases, enough history, new vs increased expenses, recurring small expenses, duplicate-looking transactions, subscription price rises, repeated alerts for the same event; thresholds justified and tested.
A.5 Insight cards on mobile and desktop: clear what/why/numbers/next step; prioritise; no duplicates, no excess notifications, no jargon, no alarming language; separate important warnings from optional suggestions.
A.6 Transparency and control: users can inspect calculations and assumptions; dismissing never modifies records; recommendations never change transactions, balances, bills, debts or goals automatically; editing sources refreshes dependent insights; stale insights are recalculated or flagged; the user stays in control.

## 4. Workstream B: investigate six additional systems (many may overlap existing features; decide which exist, which need big improvement, which are new)
B.1 Predictive cash flow: predict shortages, periods of pressure, recurring income patterns, seasonal expenses, future cash availability, income/obligation gaps, scenario effects on liquidity. Separate history from confirmed obligations; predicted income not guaranteed; account for completeness and freshness; explain assumptions; no false precision; extend the existing forecast engine, do not duplicate it.
B.2 Behavioural finance: recurring habits, rising discretionary spending, end-of-month patterns, changes in discipline, categories regularly over their norm, patterns hurting goals, improvement over time. Enough history; correlation is not causation; no judgmental language; no psychological diagnosis or unsupported claims; show the evidence.
B.3 Goal optimisation: split money between goals, debt, obligations, emergency reserve and priorities; compare contributions; completion dates; simulate extra debt payments; trade-offs of debt vs saving. Respect obligations and available funds; surplus is not all disposable; show alternatives and limits; never move money automatically.
B.4 Leak detection: forgotten subscriptions, repeated small expenses, possible duplicate charges, increased recurring costs, changing service costs, slow drains, unusual recurring payments. Confirmed vs suspected; not calling a legitimate purchase a duplicate without evidence; show the transactions behind each finding; recurring does not mean unnecessary; user can dismiss or correct.
B.5 Scenario simulator: buying a car, loan, job loss or change, extra income, moving, more savings, more debt repayment, unexpected expense, changed spending. Explain immediate impact, obligations, goals, cash flow, shortfalls, alternatives. Never modify real records; assumptions visible; simulated vs actual clearly separated; compare scenarios; long projections are not guarantees.
B.6 Resilience: emergency reserve coverage, essential monthly spending, how long reserves cover essentials, income interruption, concentration of obligations, single-income dependence (only if the data supports it), weaknesses. Reserves are not ordinary balances; not every account is immediately accessible; explain what counts as essential; identify missing information; no arbitrary health score; practical, not alarming.

## 5. Workstream C: one coherent engine
Do not build six assistants. Prefer one Financial Intelligence engine: shared calculation logic and data, common forecast assumptions, consistent dates and currency, shared completeness/freshness checks, consistent explanations, reusable scenario logic, no duplicated calculations or conflicting advice. Review the relationship of Financial Intelligence, Coach, Smart Helper, Analytics, Budgeting, Goals, Debts and loans, Recurring bills, Safe to Spend and dashboards; consolidate overlaps only where it measurably improves reliability, maintainability or experience. No refactor for elegance alone.

## 6. Workstream D: independent verification
For each major calculation: document inputs, compute the expected result by hand, show the reasoning, compare with the app, investigate differences, add regression tests for confirmed defects, test boundaries and invalid inputs. Scenarios: normal, irregular, insufficient, conflicting or outdated data, extreme values, boundary dates, edited and deleted sources, several obligations at once. Check consistency across screens. Inspect existing tests for vacuous assertions and copied logic. A passing build is not proof; a test file is not evidence it ran; report real results.

## 7. Workstream E: performance, security, maintainability
Large histories, needless recalculation, stale caches, duplicate processing, memory, reliability after edits and deletes, consistency across screens, malformed or incomplete data, privacy. Prefer local deterministic calculation. No external AI, cloud processing or sending of private financial data without a clear privacy reason and explicit user authorisation. Machine learning is not assumed necessary.

## 8. Execution
Phase 1 inspect and classify (ALREADY IMPLEMENTED AND WORKING, IMPLEMENTED BUT NEEDS IMPROVEMENT, CONFIRMED DEFECT, MISSING REQUIREMENT, GENUINELY NEW CAPABILITY, SUSPECTED RISK, OPTIONAL IMPROVEMENT); identify overlaps. Phase 2 prioritise by benefit, financial impact, reliability, dependencies, data availability, complexity, privacy and security, testing needs; correctness defects first, then improving existing trust and usefulness, then the strongest new capabilities. Phase 3 implement the justified, safe improvements, a coherent first stage of any larger system rather than half of everything. Phase 4 test: run tests, fix, add regression tests, hand-calculated verification, check screens. Phase 5 document: checkpoint, evidence log, branch and commit, files inspected, changes, tests executed, hand-calculation results, remaining work, limits, risks, exact next action.

## 9. Statuses and final report
Use the six project statuses; never VERIFIED just because it exists or builds. Final report: existing capabilities inspected; what already worked; confirmed defects and impact; improvements made; assessment of the six systems; which are genuinely new; which overlap; which are feasible in the current architecture; tests actually run and results; hand-calculated cases; limits and risks; updated checkpoint; exact next task.

## 10. Principle
Intelligence grows as the user builds history. Help users understand their position, recognise patterns, anticipate problems, compare decisions, improve habits, manage goals, prepare for surprises. Add intelligence only where it gives real value, can be built reliably and verified; do not limit the product to what exists; build, verify, learn, improve, repeat.
